# Brand resources naming audit

An audit of Compass Design Studio's brand glossary and logo file names, checked against how established organizations name the same things. All sources are first-party: the brand owner's own guideline pages, or the platform's own help center. Every page was read on **20 October 2026**.

## How to read this

- **Core sample (13 organizations).** These logo pages could be read in full: GitHub, Atlassian, Mailchimp (Intuit), Dropbox, Spotify, Stripe, Shopify, LinkedIn, Discord, IBM, MIT, UC Berkeley and Stanford. Every "x of 13" count covers only these 13.
- **Supplementary sources.** Other first-party guides fill gaps the core sample leaves (for example "descriptor" and "knockout") and supply file names you can see on the page: Figma, Slack, UC Santa Barbara, Dartmouth, University of North Florida (UNF), Quilter, University of Calgary, EFF, Python Software Foundation, Node.js, CNCF (Kubernetes), University of Nebraska Omaha (UNO), University of Groningen, Roanoke College, University of Glasgow and Frontify. These never count toward the core tallies.
- **Not used:**
  - Google's partner hub puts its guidance behind a sign-in.
  - Airbnb's media page has no logo vocabulary.
  - The Smithsonian, University of Michigan and Mastercard pages blocked automated reading (HTTP 403).
  - Stanford's download page needs a Stanford login.
  - The Mozilla and Uber portals show no readable text without scripts.
  - Harvard's guide timed out.
  - For Brandfolder, the search found only marketing blog posts, which the brief excludes.
- **No zip packages were downloaded.** The file names in section 3 come from files that pages link one by one, plus the names of the zip packages themselves.
- **How counting works.** A count means "this organization uses the term on its page." Some organizations use two terms for the same thing, so a tally can add up to more than 13.

---

## 1. Summary

- **Brand resources: keep.** None of the 13 uses it as a page name. The common page names are "brand guidelines" (4 of 13) and "brand assets" (3 of 13). Slack and Figma do call their downloads "resources" in their page copy. Put "logos" and "brand guidelines" in the page title and description so search finds the page.
- **Mark: keep, but define it.** "Marks" works as an umbrella mainly in trademark text (Figma, Discord, Slack, Dropbox, Stanford), where it also covers the typed name. The design sections of guides mostly use "logo(s)" as the umbrella. MIT and UNO title their sections "Logos & Marks", and Compass can do the same.
- **Wordmark: keep.** 8 of 13 say "wordmark" (Dropbox writes "word mark"), and 3 of 13 say "logotype". No source names a version "alt". Atlassian uses "alt" only for alt text, so continue to avoid it.
- **Descriptor: keep, and define it.** 0 of 13 use "descriptor" or "tagline". Atlassian calls its secondary line a "strapline". Outside the sample, UNF and Quilter use "descriptor" in exactly Compass's sense. No source used "tagline".
- **Combomark: rename to "lockup"** ("horizontal lockup", "stacked lockup"). 4 of 13 say "lockup", as do 6 supplementary guides. 1 of 13 (Stanford) says "combination" or "combo marks". No source writes "combomark".
- **Glyph: keep, and define it.** No term has a majority for the symbol on its own: "icon" and "glyph" (Dropbox, Atlassian) have 2 of 13 each, and 7 other terms appear once each. Watch for one clash: Figma's help center uses "glyphs" to mean the letter outlines that text becomes on export.
- **Emblem: rename to "tile"** (or keep it with a firm one-line definition). 0 sources use "emblem" for a symbol on a square, and the one that uses "emblem" at all (Stanford) means a crest or seal. Atlassian describes this exact construction as a glyph inside a "tile".
- **Ground: rename to "background".** 10 of 13 say "background(s)"; 0 say "ground".
- **Clear space and minimum size: keep both.** "Clear space" appears in 8 of 13; Discord writes "Clearspace" and Spotify says "exclusion zone". "Minimum size" appears word for word in 5 of 13, and 6 of 13 give both a print and a screen value. Compass should give both too.
- **Files and gaps.**
  - Add the `compass-` prefix to the tile files: 6 of 7 packages with visible file names start with the brand name.
  - Keep lowercase with hyphens (4 of 7 packages).
  - Leave colorspace out of file names (only 1 of 7 includes it).
  - Add a black one-color version: 9 of 13 offer black.
  - Figma's help center documents no CMYK export, only sRGB and Display P3.

### 2.1 Brand resources (the site's name)

**What the sources call the page:**
- **"Brand guidelines":** 4 of 13.
  - [Dropbox][dbx] says "Brand Guidelines".
  - [Spotify][spo] says "Design & Branding Guidelines".
  - [LinkedIn][li] says "Brand Guidelines".
  - [Discord][dis] says "Brand Guidelines".
- **"Brand assets":** 3 of 13.
  - [Mailchimp][mc] uses it as the page heading.
  - [Stripe][str] uses it in the URL; the page title is "Information and assets".
  - [Shopify][shp] uses it in the URL and in its "Download all brand assets" link.
- **"Guide":** 2 of 13, [MIT][mit] (Brand Guide) and [Stanford][su-logos] (Identity Guide).
- **"Toolkit" or "kit":** 1 of 13 as a page name, [GitHub][gh] (Brand Toolkit). [Atlassian][atl] also has a "Brand kit" footer link, and [Discord][dis] has a "View Brand Kit" button.
- **A design-system site:** 2 of 13, [Atlassian][atl] (Atlassian Design) and [IBM][ibm] (IBM Design Language).
- **Just "Brand":** 1 of 13, [UC Berkeley][ucb] (UC Berkeley Brand).

**"Brand resources" itself:** 0 of 13 use it as a page name. In page copy, though:
- [Slack][slack]'s Media Kit calls its logo files and screenshots resources for using its brand.
- [Figma][figma-brand]'s brand page points readers to "brand resources" for downloads.
- [Mailchimp][mc]'s page title is "Brand Assets and Resources Guidelines".

**Neighboring terms.** The platform [Frontify][frontify] calls this kind of site a "brand portal". [Slack][slack] calls its full site a "Brand Center" and a "hub". [Spotify][spo] calls its page a "hub for partner guidelines and assets".

**Recommendation: keep "Brand resources".** It is plain English, it covers both the files and the rules, and no standard says otherwise. Visitors are likely to search for "logo" and "brand guidelines", so put those words in the page title and meta description. The avoid-list terms are not wrong; Compass simply didn't choose them. "Brand kit" has relatives at GitHub, Atlassian and Discord, and "hub" appears at Slack and Spotify.

### 2.2 Mark (umbrella term)

**Where "mark(s)" is the umbrella:** mostly in legal or trademark text, where it includes the typed name as well as the artwork.
- [Figma][figma-brand] defines "Figma marks" as its words, logos, graphics and other source indicators.
- [Discord][dis] groups its name and the Clyde logo as "Discord Marks".
- [Slack][slack] defines "Slack marks" as the Slack name, its logo, and any word or image identifying Slack.
- [Dropbox][dbx2] talks about its "word marks" (spelling, capitalization).
- [Stanford][su-logos] says the trademark symbol helps protect "the marks", and lists slogans like "Cardinal" as word marks.

**Where "logo(s)" is the umbrella:** in the design sections.
- [Atlassian][atl] defines a logo as a word, an image, or both.
- [Stanford][su-logos] says its logo system includes wordmarks, the Block S, the seal and their combinations.
- [GitHub][gh] says its color guidance applies to all its logos.

**Where both appear:** [MIT][mit] heads its section "Logos & Marks", and so does [UNO][uno]. [UC Berkeley][ucb-icons] has a page called "Icons & secondary marks". [GitHub][gh] calls the Invertocat its "primary mark" and events get "Event marks".

**Where "logo" names one version:** this is common. Examples are [Spotify][spo]'s "full logo", [Shopify][shp]'s "Primary logo" and [Berkeley][ucb]'s "Primary logo". Compass's rule against using "logo" for one specific version is a deliberate choice; most sources don't follow it.

**Recommendation: keep "mark", but define it in one line.** For example: "Mark: any of our logo artwork (the wordmark, lockups, glyph and tiles)." Note that in trademark use "marks" also covers the plain typed name "Compass", which isn't a download. Use "Logos & marks" as the navigation label or heading, because non-designers look for "logo".

### 2.3 Wordmark (and its versions)

**The term:**
- **"wordmark":** 8 of 13. [GitHub][gh], [Atlassian][atl], [Dropbox][dbx] ("word mark"), [Spotify][spo], [Stripe][str], [Shopify][shp], [Stanford][su-wm] and [UC Berkeley][ucb-sig] (in its lockup colorways).
- **"logotype":** 3 of 13. [GitHub][gh] uses it for its past versions, [IBM][ibm] for the 8-bar, and [UC Berkeley][ucb] for its secondary athletic marks.
- **Definitions:** [Atlassian][atl] defines a wordmark as the name of the product or property. [Stanford][su-wm] tells users to treat its wordmark as artwork, not as typography or a font, because no typeface reproduces it.

**Version names.** Most organizations name a version by its color:
- [Stripe][str]: slate, blurple, white.
- [Spotify][spo]: green, black, white.
- [GitHub][gh]: white, black, grey, green.
- [Discord][dis]: color, black, white.
- [Stanford][su-wm]: Cardinal red, white, black.

**The light version for dark backgrounds:**
- **"white":** 9 of 13. GitHub, Atlassian (its property logos turn white on dark backgrounds), Spotify, Stripe, Shopify ("Monotone white"), LinkedIn, Discord, MIT and Berkeley.
- **"reverse" or "reversed":** 3 of 13. [Mailchimp][mc], [IBM][ibm] and [Stanford][su-wm].
- **"inverted":** 1 of 13, [Shopify][shp].
- **"inverse":** 1 of 13, [Atlassian][atl] (its color options are Brand, inverse and neutral).
- **"negative":** used once, by [IBM][ibm], alongside "reversed".
- **"knockout":** 0 of 13. Among supplementary sources, [Roanoke][roanoke] says "reversed/knockout", [UC Santa Barbara][ucsb] says "reversed (white)", and [EFF][eff] says its lockup is "reversed out" against black.

**"Alt":** 0 sources use it to name a version. [Atlassian][atl] uses "alt-text" many times in its accessibility sense, which is a good reason to keep "alt" out of version names.

**Recommendations:**
- **Keep "wordmark".** Define it as "COMPASS in our lettering, with the glyph standing in for the A. It is artwork: never retype it."
- **Keep "paper"** as the name of the light version, because naming by color follows the majority. Paper isn't white, though, and 9 of 13 sources say "white". Label the download card "Paper, for dark backgrounds". When talking to printers, "reversed" is the established word for this job (3 of 13, plus 3 supplementary sources).
- **Check the optical weight of the paper version.** [IBM][ibm] redraws its reversed logo, with thinner white stripes and blunter points, so it looks the same weight on dark backgrounds.
- **Rename "gold-glyph" in file names to `navy-gold-glyph`.** Sources name a two-color version by listing its colors and which part each color goes on: [CNCF][cncf-grad] uses `white-text`, [UNO][uno] uses `color-white`, and [Berkeley][ucb-sig] writes out which color the wordmark is and which color the unit name is.

### 2.4 Descriptor

**What the sources call the secondary words:**
- **Core sample:** 0 of 13 use "descriptor" or "tagline".
- [Atlassian][atl] calls its secondary line a "strapline". In its case that is a list of app names set under the logo, and its downloads list "straplines or other secondary text" as a logo element.
- [MIT][mit-lockups] locks its logo to the Institute's full name. [UC Berkeley][ucb-sig] locks its logo to a unit name.
- [Dartmouth][dartmouth] describes a lockup as a logo plus accompanying elements, such as the department name.

**Supplementary sources using "descriptor" in Compass's sense:**
- [UNF][unf] says its official logo has three parts: monogram, descriptor and Osprey image. Its descriptor is the spelled-out university name, custom-drawn, and UNF says not to retype it with a font.
- [Quilter][quilter] calls its descriptor a visual asset that describes the group's business, used mainly in a lockup with the logo, in stacked and horizontal versions.

**"Tagline":** none of the sources read for this audit used it.

**Recommendation: keep "descriptor".** "Design Studio" describes what Compass is, which matches the UNF and Quilter usage. Define it as "the words DESIGN STUDIO in spaced capitals, beside or under the wordmark. Part of the artwork: never retype it."

### 2.5 Combomark: rename to "lockup"

**What the sources call wordmark plus descriptor (or name plus symbol):**
- **"lockup" or "lock-up":** 4 of 13.
  - [GitHub][gh]: "Wordmark lockup" and "Product lockups".
  - [Atlassian][atl]: attribution logos are "a type of lockup", and its downloads include lockups with wordmarks.
  - [MIT][mit-lockups]: "Logo Lock-Ups", in one-line and three-line versions.
  - [UC Berkeley][ucb-sig]: "Horizontal lockup" and "Stacked lockup".
- **"lockup" in supplementary sources:** 6 more.
  - [UC Santa Barbara][ucsb]: "wordmark lock-ups".
  - [Dartmouth][dartmouth].
  - [UNF][unf]: sub-branded lockups.
  - [University of Calgary][ucalgary].
  - [Quilter][quilter]: its "logo lockup" combines the logo with the primary descriptor, in stacked and horizontal versions. This is the closest match to Compass's combomark.
  - [EFF][eff].
- **"full logo":** 2 of 13, [Spotify][spo] and [Dropbox][dbx].
- **"primary logo" or just "logo":** 4 of 13, [Shopify][shp], [LinkedIn][li], [Discord][dis] and [Mailchimp][mc].
- **"Combinations", "combination logos" or "combo marks":** 1 of 13, [Stanford][su-combo]. The supplementary [Python][python] page uses "Combined logo".
- **No combined mark at all:** [Stripe][str] and [IBM][ibm].
- **"combomark" written as one word:** 0 sources.
- **"signature":** used by [Stanford][su-wm] (the wordmark itself, plus "department signature" and "unit signatures") and [UC Berkeley][ucb-sig] ("Signature system"). [Dropbox][dbx2] uses it loosely, calling its logo a universal signature. Both Berkeley and Stanford also have "Email Signatures" pages in their site navigation, so the word already means something else to staff.

**Recommendation: rename "combomark" to "lockup".** Use "horizontal lockup" and "stacked lockup", as [UC Berkeley][ucb-sig] and [Quilter][quilter] do. For non-designers, define it in plain words, as [Dartmouth][dartmouth] does: a fixed arrangement of the wordmark and the descriptor that is never rearranged. If Compass wants to avoid design jargon entirely, the plain alternative is "full logo" ([Spotify][spo], [Dropbox][dbx]). That clashes with Compass's own rule against using "logo" for one version.

### 2.6 Glyph

**What the sources call the symbol on its own:**

| Term | Orgs (core) |
|---|---|
| icon | 2: [Spotify][spo]; [Mailchimp][mc] ("Freddie icon"). [Atlassian][atl] also defines its logomark as the icon portion of a logo. |
| glyph | 2: [Dropbox][dbx] (glyph plus word mark); [Atlassian][atl] (each app's glyph sits in a tile) |
| logomark | 1: [Atlassian][atl] |
| symbol | 1: [Discord][dis] |
| brandmark | 1: [Shopify][shp] |
| pictogram | 1: [GitHub][gh] |
| "[in] Logo" | 1: [LinkedIn][li] |
| emblem | 1: [Stanford][su-logos] (the Block S with tree) |
| monogram | 1: [UC Berkeley][ucb-icons] (the "B") |
| no separate symbol | 3: [Stripe][str], [IBM][ibm], [MIT][mit] |

**Supplementary sources:**
- "icon": [CNCF][cncf-readme] and [UNO][uno].
- "logo device": [Python][python], for the two snakes without the wordmark.
- "monogram": [EFF][eff] and [UNF][unf], both for letters joined together.

**Why the avoided terms are fair to avoid:**
- **"icon"** is overloaded. [IBM][ibm]'s design language has separate sections for UI icons, app icons and pictograms. [Spotify][spo] uses "icon" both for its symbol and for the app icon on a phone.
- **"monogram"** in these sources usually means joined letters ([UNF][unf], [EFF][eff]), although Berkeley uses it for a single "B".

**One clash to know about.** [Figma's help center][figma-formats] says that "Outline text" converts text layers into glyphs, meaning letter outlines. Studio staff reading Figma's docs could trip over this.

**Recommendation: keep "glyph".** Two of 13 use it for exactly this role, and no alternative has a majority. Define it as "the compass-A symbol on its own." On the download card, consider a plain helper label such as "Glyph (symbol)".

### 2.7 Emblem: rename to "tile"

**What Compass means:** the glyph centered on a square tile of fixed color.

**Sources describing this construction:**
- [Atlassian][atl]'s app logos are a glyph contained in a tile, and the tile's color signals the app's collection. For an app, Atlassian says "logomark" refers to the tile, and it fixes the corner radius on those tile backgrounds.
- [Dropbox][dbx]'s "Tab" is the glyph inside a plane.
- [University of Glasgow][glasgow] (supplementary) offers a "boxed logo" and an "unboxed logo", and requires the boxed version on University blue when the logo sits over an image.
- [Spotify][spo] allows its icon alone when it serves as an app icon.

**How sources use "emblem":** [Stanford][su-combo] calls the university seal and the Block S with tree its "emblems". On its [logos overview][su-logos] it applies clear space to all "logo, wordmark and emblem variations". Those are crest-type marks. 0 sources use "emblem" for a symbol on a square.

**How sources name the uses of a square mark:**
- **Favicon:** [MIT][mit-logo] offers its favicon as a separate download, in black or white only. [UC Berkeley][ucb-icons] says to use the "B" monogram as the website favicon, which treats the favicon as a use of an existing mark, just as Compass does.
- **Social accounts:** [MIT][mit-social] calls these "social media icons". [UC Berkeley][ucb-icons] says "social media icons", "web icons" and "profile image".
- **"Avatar":** 0 sources.
- **"Badge":** [Stripe][str] uses it for its "Powered by Stripe" badge, which other businesses display. That is a different thing, so Compass is right to avoid "badge".

**Recommendation: rename "emblem" to "tile",** following [Atlassian][atl]'s glyph-in-a-tile wording, and describe favicons and social profile images as uses of a tile. The evidence for "tile" is one direct precedent. The evidence against "emblem" is that no source uses it this way and the one source that uses it means a crest or seal. If Compass keeps "emblem", define it explicitly: "the glyph on a colored square".

### 2.8 Ground: rename to "background"

**What the sources say:**
- **"background(s)":** 10 of 13. [GitHub][gh], [Atlassian][atl], [Mailchimp][mc], [Spotify][spo], [Stripe][str], [Shopify][shp], [LinkedIn][li], [IBM][ibm], [MIT][mit-logo] and [Stanford][su-wm].
- **"ground" on its own:** 0 of 13.
- **Background lightness:** most sources simply say light or dark. [Stripe][str] uses slate and blurple on light backgrounds and white on dark ones, and [Spotify][spo] and [Stanford][su-wm] work the same way.
- **Named color pairs:** [UC Berkeley][ucb] uses pairs like "White on Berkeley Blue", and [MIT][mit-logo] calls its red-on-red versions "Red-on-red treatments".

**Recommendation:** say "light backgrounds" and "dark backgrounds". Replace "ink grounds" in particular: Ink is also one of Compass's color names, and printers will read "ink" literally (this second point is a judgment call, not a sourced finding). In file names, use `-on-{color}` for color pairs, following Berkeley and MIT.

### 2.9 Clear space

**What the sources call it:**
- **"clear space":** 8 of 13. [Atlassian][atl], [Shopify][shp], [LinkedIn][li], [IBM][ibm], [MIT][mit-logo], [UC Berkeley][ucb], [Stanford][su-logos], and [GitHub][gh] (only as a diagram label, "Invertocat clear space guide").
- **"Clearspace" as one word:** 1 of 13, [Discord][dis].
- **"exclusion zone":** 1 of 13, [Spotify][spo].
- **Synonyms used alongside "clear space":** [Stanford][su-wm] says "buffer zone", [IBM][ibm] says "area of isolation" and [Atlassian][atl] says "clearance".
- **No rule:** [Mailchimp][mc] only asks for plenty of space, and Dropbox and Stripe say nothing.

**How it is measured:** usually from a piece of the mark.
- [Atlassian][atl]: the capital A of its wordmark.
- [MIT][mit-logo]: the width of the M.
- [UC Berkeley][ucb]: the letter e.
- [Stanford][su-wm]: the x-height of "Stanford".
- [Shopify][shp]: the x-height of the wordmark, or half the S for the bag.
- [Spotify][spo]: half the icon's height.
- [LinkedIn][li]: one [in] logo.
- [IBM][ibm]: the logo's height.

**Recommendation: keep "clear space"** (two words). Measure it from the glyph. Like Atlassian's capital A, Compass's A is its glyph.

### 2.10 Minimum size

**What the sources say:**
- **"Minimum size" word for word:** 5 of 13. [Spotify][spo], [Shopify][shp], [LinkedIn][li], [UC Berkeley][ucb] and [Stanford][su-wm].
- **Limits stated without the phrase:**
  - [Atlassian][atl]: don't scale logos with straplines below 280 px wide.
  - [MIT][mit-logo]: standard logo above 0.25 in or 30 px tall, and a separate "micro logo" below that.
- **Both a print and a screen value:** 6 of 13.

| Org | Print value | Screen value |
|---|---|---|
| Spotify | 20 mm (icon 6 mm) | 70 px (icon 21 px) |
| Shopify | 28 mm | 80 px |
| LinkedIn | 0.25 in | 21 px |
| Berkeley | 1.75 in | 200 px |
| Stanford | 0.85 in | 85 px |
| MIT | 0.25 in | 30 px |

- **Favicon:** [UC Berkeley][ucb-icons] sets a 16 × 16 px minimum.

**Recommendation: keep "minimum size".** Give each mark a print value in mm or inches and a screen value in px. For tiny uses such as favicons, MIT's micro logo is a precedent for drawing simplified artwork.

### 2.11 Primary and secondary (missing from the glossary)

**"Primary":** 6 of 13 name a primary mark.
- [GitHub][gh]: the Invertocat is its primary logo.
- [Spotify][spo]: its green logo is the "primary logo colorway".
- [Shopify][shp]: "Primary logo".
- [MIT][mit-logo]: its logo is the "primary graphic symbol".
- [UC Berkeley][ucb]: "Primary logo".
- [Stanford][su-logos]: the wordmark is the university's primary logo.

**"Secondary":**
- [UC Berkeley][ucb-icons] has secondary marks and logotypes.
- [Shopify][shp] uses the bag alone as a secondary brand signifier.
- [Stanford][su-wm] allows black for secondary use.

**Suggestion:** state which Compass mark is primary, so contractors know what to reach for first.

### 2.12 Black one-color version (gap)

**What the sources offer:** 9 of 13 provide a black version.
- [GitHub][gh], [Spotify][spo], [LinkedIn][li], [Discord][dis] and [MIT][mit-logo].
- [Shopify][shp] ("Monotone black").
- [IBM][ibm] (its positive-logo examples include black).
- [UC Berkeley][ucb] (a "One-color logo" in black only, for black-and-white and grayscale work).
- [Stanford][su-wm] (black for black-and-white and grayscale).

[Shopify][shp] lists single-color printing budgets as one reason to use the monotone logo. [Stripe][str], by contrast, forbids any color but its three.

**Terms:** "one-color" ([Berkeley][ucb]), "monotone" ([Shopify][shp]), "monochrome" ([Spotify][spo]), "1color" ([EFF][eff] file names) and "single colour" ([Glasgow][glasgow]).

**Recommendation:** add black versions for black-and-white and single-color printing: `compass-wordmark-black`, black lockups and a black glyph. Compass already has single-color navy and paper wordmarks.

### 2.13 Color names (brief)

**What the sources do:** they pair each brand color name with print specs.
- [Stanford][su-logos] lists Cardinal red as Pantone 201 C and tells licensed vendors to match the Pantone colors.
- [Groningen][rug] gives PMS, CMYK and RGB values for its red, and warns that automatic conversions differ from the official CMYK.

**Recommendation:** Figma cannot produce CMYK files (section 4.1). So list HEX/RGB, CMYK and, where one exists, a Pantone value for every Compass color, so printers and sign shops can match.

**Two consistency notes** (judgment calls, not tallies):
- The word order of the modifiers mixes "Navy deep" and "Gold deep" with "Light gold". Putting the base color first ("Gold light") keeps related colors together in lists.
- "Ink" and "Navy ink" are two different colors with similar names.

### 3.2 Recommended pattern for Compass

```
compass-{mark}[-{layout}]-{color}[-on-{background color}].{svg|png|pdf}
```

1. **Start every file with `compass-`** (6 of 7 packages). This fixes the tile files that currently lack the prefix, and keeps all files grouped together in a downloads folder.
2. **Lowercase, hyphens only, no spaces** (4 of 7). This also avoids encoded characters like MIT's `%2B`.
3. **`{mark}`** is the glossary word: `wordmark`, `lockup`, `glyph` or `tile`.
4. **`{layout}`** applies to lockups only: `horizontal` or `stacked` ([UC Berkeley][ucb-sig], [CNCF][cncf-grad], [Node.js][nodejs]).
5. **`{color}`** is the color of the artwork itself, using the site's color names with hyphens (`navy`, `paper`, `navy-ink`, `light-gold`). For two-color artwork, give the main color, then the accent and the part it colors (`navy-gold-glyph`).
6. **Add `-on-{color}` only when the background is part of the artwork**, which means the tiles (Berkeley's color pairs; IBM's and MIT's illustration files).
7. **Leave colorspace out of Figma exports.** They are all RGB (section 4.1). If CMYK or Pantone files are ever produced outside Figma, add `-cmyk` or `-pantone` at the end of the name, following [UNO][uno].
8. **Use one base name for .svg, .png and .pdf.** On the page, say which format is for what job, as UNO and Groningen do.

**Practical Figma tips** (all from [Export static designs from Figma][figma-export] and [Export formats and settings][figma-formats]):
- **Layer name becomes file name.** Figma names an exported file after its layer, so name each export frame exactly as the target file (`compass-wordmark-navy`).
- **Slashes become folders.** A slash in a layer name creates nested folders. A layer named `wordmark/compass-wordmark-navy` would export as `compass-wordmark-navy` inside a `wordmark` folder.
- **The Suffix field adds no separator.** Whatever you type in Figma's optional Suffix field is appended directly, with no hyphen (Figma's own example produces `HomePagedraft.png`). Type the hyphen yourself if you use it.

## 4. Figma export facts (Figma Help Center only)

### 4.1 Can Figma export CMYK files?

**No, as far as the Help Center documents.**
- [Manage color profiles in design files][figma-profiles]: Figma Design offers exactly two color profiles, sRGB and Display P3.
- [Export formats and settings][figma-formats]: the export color-profile dropdown offers "Same as file", sRGB and Display P3. PNG exports are 32-bit RGBA.
- [Export assets from Figma Buzz][figma-buzz] and [Export from Figma Slides][figma-slides]: PDF exports from Buzz and from Slides offer only sRGB or Display P3.
- [About color models][figma-models]: the color picker's models are Hex, HSB, HSL, CSS and RGB. CMYK is not among them.
- The only Help Center article that matched a search for "CMYK" is [Built by the Figma team: shaders and plugins][figma-shaders]. There, a halftone effect's CMYK mode imitates print dot patterns. It is a visual effect, not a color-space export.

**Implication** (inference, not a Help Center statement): printer-ready CMYK or Pantone files must be made in other software, or by the printer. Hence the advice in section 2.13 to publish CMYK and Pantone values.

### 4.2 Are PDF exports vector? Does text stay live?

According to [Export formats and settings][figma-formats]:

- **Vector.** Figma's PDFs contain text, fonts, vector graphics and images in a fixed layout, saved as PDF 1.7. Vector shapes stay vector. PDF export works only at 1x, embedded images default to Medium quality, and the "Plus darker" and "Plus lighter" blend modes aren't supported.
- **Text.** Figma exports PDF text "as glyphs", so the text can't be edited in the PDF, but it can still be selected and copied in a PDF viewer. Text you can select and copy is still real text, not converted outlines (that is an inference from the article).
- **No outline option.** The article's settings table shows "Outline text" is not available for PDF.
- **Recommendation for files going to printers and sign shops:** flatten the text first (section 4.4) so no font is needed at the other end.

### 4.3 What the SVG export options do

All from [Export formats and settings][figma-formats]:

- **Outline text.** Turned on by default when at least one text layer is selected. It converts text layers into glyphs (letter outlines) so the appearance holds, and the text can't be edited after export. Turn it off to keep text editable.
- **Include "id" attribute.** Adds an `id` to the SVG, based on the layer's name in the Layers panel. Code (JavaScript or a stylesheet) can then target that element.
- **Simplify stroke.** Turned on by default for vector networks with an inside or outside stroke. SVG supports only center strokes, so this setting makes other software draw inside and outside strokes correctly. If you turn it off, Figma thickens the stroke and adds a mask instead: it looks the same but adds code.
- **Also documented:**
  - SVG export works only at 1x.
  - Strokes are exported as fills.
  - Background blurs must be applied to the layer itself.
  - "Ignore overlapping layers" and "Include bounding box" (for text layers) are also available.

### 4.4 How to flatten text to vector shapes before export

From [Convert text to vector paths][figma-text-to-vector] and [Flatten layers][figma-flatten]. There are two methods, and both are destructive: the text can no longer be edited. Undo, or the file's version history, gets the text back.

- **Flatten.** Select the text, right-click and choose **Flatten**, or press **Option Shift F** (Mac) or **Alt Shift F** (Windows). The selection becomes a single vector layer.
- **Outline stroke.** Right-click and choose **Outline stroke**, or press **Command Option O** (Mac) or **Control Alt O** (Windows). Each letter becomes its own vector layer.

The Help Center lists "preparing assets for printing" and "creating logos or wordmarks" as uses of this conversion. Keep an unflattened master copy of each mark, and export from flattened duplicates.
